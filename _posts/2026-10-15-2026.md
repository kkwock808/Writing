Hello, I figured that since I was about to hit the big 3-0, I should do a decade review! However, there is only so much I can write here, and I will do my best to highlight me, the places I lived, and the world around me at these moments in time. Nothing more exciting than taking a stroll down memory lane with you:

## 2016: Cleveland, a rust belt experience

Among the very first places I've lived outside of Hawaii, Cleveland was also a place of growth, self-discovery, and grit. Here, I spent some four long years completing my degrees in Engineering Physics and Music. While not the most sparkling city of the Midwest, Cleveland punches above its own weight. The world's best hospital is arguably based in Cleveland, as is the world's best orchestra. Thankfully, I was able to call this place college and home for four years. And I think junior year, right around this time, was kicking my ass… But I still kept my social calendar busy with friends and fun from the music department!

## 2017: Dipping my toes with a congressional visit

It was around April 2017, and congressional visits in Washington, DC were at their peak, with constituents lobbying their representatives for funding. During Orange Man’s first term, I visited Rep. Tim Ryan’s office to advocate for science funding, noting that Orange Man sought to reduce it at all levels! While this was lobbying in a way, it was completely non-for-profit and a healthy reminder to representatives that we, the people, still and will hold them accountable for their votes on budget proposals in the next few months.

## 2018: Senior year of college with House 5 and... NYC!

In the brisk hustle of senior year, I lived in House 5. I lost touch with a few of my housemates in House 5 but still got my main girlies around! I’m so, so happy I still video call them every now and then, especially since I haven’t been back to the Midwest for quite some time now. I think right around this time, around January 2018, I had no idea about what I would be doing later at the end of the year. Chaos and unpredictability didn’t just stay in college; they followed me right up until…

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2018_1.jpg">

New York City! Here, I met more friends as a first-year student at Columbia University. Part of why I am here in Europe is probably this cute little bunch of friends. However, none of this would have been possible were it not for Columbia University, the International House, and a bunch of extroverted nerds that wanted to hang out past midnight on a school night, in the tiny rooms of I-House South, 8th floor.

## 2020: Change is sometimes too quick and instantaneous

Among the last few dwellers in the International House, things were dire in early March 2020. Jean, Stefan, and I were among the last residents evicted from the dorm because living conditions simply weren't adequate during a worldwide pandemic in hotspot NYC. It is a tough situation, but I get it. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2020_5.jpg">

I had the option to go back home to Hawaii, but chose to stay in NYC hoping to ride out the pandemic. What started as a 2-week shutdown became a year-long period of uncertainty. On the off chance I was in the lab, I always went down the Northwest Corner Building stairwell and took pictures of the Manhattan skyline. Oh, I miss this!

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2020_4.jpg">

Additionally, I even chose not to go home for winter break that year. I just went to Philip’s house in Okemos, Michigan, to experience my very first Midwestern Christmas. Staying at the Harrisons was a delight and truly a home away from home with the family of 30+ kids that year! Here we were in the cozy house of Aunty Laura's, playing chess while the weather snowed us in. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2020_3.jpg">

## 2021: The Great American Road Trip(s)

After Christmas with the Harrisons, Philip needed to get out west to the West Coast, so I opted to join in for the fun! Here, we bounced through almost every national park in the Southwest, and I must say, I think I have a complicated relationship with the Southwest! Vast nature, never-ending horizons, and eternal sunny days were common throughout this road trip (a necessity considering we were still in a pandemic!). Complicated because I spent so much time in New Mexico and never really truly appreciated states outside of NM. Here, we were experiencing the Grand Canyon at dusk, showcasing America's natural highlights in glowing orange sunset light.

## 2022: NYC is back.

With things slowly opening back up again in NYC, I had to take some pictures with the relatively newish Vanderbilt One. On a nice afternoon, one can catch all the colors of a Manhattan skyline, and moments like these were spectacular. Also, having two BFFs live in NYC was probably the most exciting chapter anyone can ask for in their 20s. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2022_1.jpeg">

What's even more amazing is that year a small Cessna my friend, Basile, flew while close to the One World Trade Center! Here I was taking a selfie with NYC’s most defining structure in its skyline. I remember this being a fairly calm spring in April, when my friend Basile (also Belgian!) decided to go flying and invited me on board. The calm wind was pretty smooth and perfect for a newbie like me to fly in a rattling tin can.

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2022_2.jpeg">

Speaking of Cessnas and exploring America, Philip took us on a tour of Southeast Alaska. Alaska has quite the daring transportation system of ferries, boat-planes, propeller planes, and even helicopters. Here, we were at the Mendenhall Glacier in Juneau. It was the July 4th weekend in 2022, and we spent the trip exploring most of SE Alaska. As I would always say that year, “I’d move to Alaska if it weren’t so damn cold”. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2022_3.jpeg">

Lastly, my last autumn in New Mexico was marked by the most beautiful Albuquerque-en scenery during a crisp morning. The 50th balloon fiesta had been on my to-do list for quite a while. Always gone too soon after my internships at Los Alamos to participate, this year I made the effort to see color orbs decorate the New Mexican dawn. The wildest thing was how involved the Air Force base was in celebrating the 50th Balloon Fiesta–we had a flyover! I highly recommend!

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2022_4.jpeg">

## 2024: Closing a chapter and starting a new one. In a different continent.

NYC usually gets a few weeks of bitter cold, on top of fresh powdery snow. When it snows, it’s usually best to go frolic before the Manhattan air quality stains the snow a light grey (or a light yellow if a dog happens to pass by…). Fresh from winter vacation, I trekked out and marked this as the last winter in NYC before graduating later this year. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2024_1.jpeg"> 

I’m always thrilled when I get invited to weddings, and Beth and Brian were the only ones I claim to be responsible for! I was roommates with Brian and Beth was my BFF, and thanks to my superb networking, they’re now married! I will always remember this day, not because I was there, but because Beth gave every one of her bridesmaids, but not me, custom Crocs. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2024_2.jpeg"> 

It was April and thesis-writing time. But on a nice April morning, I took a 6-hour drive from NYC with Flor to upstate NYC. What better thing to do if you didn’t want to write your thesis? Of course, the answer is the Great American Eclipse!

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2024_3.jpeg"> 

It was the week after defending my thesis, and I was riding a high. So high that in one of the parties held in Basile’s apartment, I fell face-first onto the floor while running for a piece of matcha cheesecake. I was miraculously not in pain because my tooth nerves were still intact, despite the tooth losing its upper half. What I felt embarrassed about was the party having to end because of the medical incident in Basile's room. EEK!

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2024_4.jpeg"> 

I graduated that Spring of 2024 surrounded by family, friends, and lab mates. It was a marathon, but it was an enjoyable one. A PhD completed in the world’s most dynamic city was something I never expected growing up on the islands of Hawaii. But here we are! A lot to be proud of, and a lot of folks to be thankful for for having made it. I think by around now, I was already set for my next chapter of life, on a completely different continent. NYC, as I’ve always told myself, will be home, and I hope to be back soon!

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/8/2024_5.jpg"> 

In July, I was going to move to Belgium to start a postdoc. But knowing me, I had to pit stop in Iceland. Scott really wanted to visit Iceland, and I guess I did too! Here we are, eating what I think was risotto and a piece of chicken. Having just moved out of my NYC dorm, everything packed into two pieces of luggage, I thought a pit stop in Iceland would be bliss, and it was! Always a treat to be traveling with friends when they’re around and free.

## 2025: Hanging on to my Euro life

Around December 2024, I still expected I wouldn’t be in Europe for long. My thoughts were: why not go to a country I would never have visited had I stayed in the US? The first country that popped into my head was Southern Africa. In this picture, we were capping off a Namibia Road trip and Garden Isle drive with a hike on Lion's Peak. My brother was (kind of) coerced into joining this trip, and I’m glad he did. We fought a little, but we also had fun (I hope) in our very own personal ways.
