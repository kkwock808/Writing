## Winter life in Ghent 

I’ve come to appreciate immensely my near-daily bike rides to work. The commute, only 30 minutes one-way, initially seemed impossible for a person who walks no more than 10 minutes to get to work (in NYC). Now, I find the hour-long bike rides relaxing and healthy for my Belgian lifestyle. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_commute.jpg">

In winter, biking can be a life hazard if not done correctly! To bike comfortably in the moist Belgian winters, I got a pair of gloves, a set of bike lights, bike seat covers, a waterproof jacket, and a helmet to bubble-wrap my body in case of a biking mishap. The tram rails are especially dangerous, and I consciously try to avoid them during my commute. 

Biking in snow is not common in Ghent simply because Ghent rarely snows! But if I know my Belgians well, snow, rain, or hailstorms, biking is still the preferred choice for commuting. But who would want to bike in the snow when you can take a bus and soak in this wintery dream?

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_winterbiking.jpeg">

## Gobsmacked in London

A convenient way to travel in Western Europe is with the Eurostar train network that links Paris, Brussels, Amsterdam, Cologne, and London. Hopping on one to London for a quick weekend trip was a breeze since the trip only took 2 hours and made a quick stop in Lille, France. London, itself, was quite expensive. A metro ticket was 2.7£, whereas a NYC subway ticket was $2.75! I guess the train network in London is well-connected, much more so than NYC, justifying the high cost! 

A memorable food experience was the Chinatown in London. There, I had to purchase my favorites: bolo bau, charsiu bao, and cream buns. On another night, Sander and I grabbed another favorite: Marugame Udon. I did not think they would have this in London, but here we were, chomping on our katsu chicken!!! Afterward, we went to a cinema to watch the Oscar-winning film The Brutalist!

## Wien

My first stop in Vienna was the Schonbrunn Palace. Here, I learned about Hofburg ruling family and their expensive taste for fine living at the peak of their empire. Each room was elaborately decorated with ornately carved wood, painted ceilings, and marble. Even more astonishing was that Maria Theresa, the matriarch of Hofburgs, gave birth to ~13 children, each married off for political gains. I enjoyed stewing in the glimmering halls of the Schönbrunn and recommend this to any visitor to Vienna!

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_schonbrun.jpeg">

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_palace.jpeg">

Apparently, markets would surround the palace area during Christmas for holiday festivities! 

Next up, the Austrian National Library was a fascinating building that housed ancient texts collected from the early 1600s. The library itself was modeled after the Renaissance style of glorious Italian arches but with little hints of modernism from the 1600s. The impressively high ceilings and marble statue of Joseph were even better to witness with the 0.5x camera setting on my iPhone.

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_austrialibrary.jpeg">

Two friends from I-House, Elias and Lisa, wanted to show me a quieter,  local side of Vienna. The Kahlbern loop hike took us around the vineyards of Northern Vienna and up to a scenic viewpoint overlooking the rest of the city. Here, we reveled in the early spring sunlight that warmed our bodies on a cold March day. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_hike.jpeg">

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_hike1.jpeg">

After coming down from the hike, we sat in a home inn. Elias says that you know that these inns (from the vineyards) are open for food and drinks when the candlelight is on outside its front entrance. I had my first taste of a local delicacy: the Schnitzel, grape juice with sparkling water, blood sausage, and Grammelschmaltz. I was privileged to have two native German-speaking Viennese friends guide me through this experience! 

The next day, a Monday, I worked a bit in the morning before bumbling off to the Belvedere Palace to witness the Kiss by Klimt. Actually, this museum specializes in the largest collection of Klimt. While it was expensive, I’m glad to have been able to witness several stunning pieces in this museum, including Klimt, the iconic painting of Napolean on a horse, and several Viennese impressionist pieces. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_klimt.jpeg">

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_belvedere.jpeg">

Lastly, I attended the opera on a Monday night. Since I had to catch a 6 am flight the following day, I almost stayed only for half of the Vienna Opera’s performance of Norma, but I was glad that I stayed the entire time. Even though I had standing-room tickets, I think it was well-spent since the experience was entirely novel. The most moving and most explainable crowd-pleaser tune of the opera was the Casta Diva Aria. Here, Norma completes her ritual and blesses her people in the peaceful night of the forest. I was completely enamored and privileged to have heard this live in the Vienna State Opera House. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_opera.jpeg">

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_opera1.jpeg">

Favorite food alert: Schnitzel! The history of schnitzel originates from Austria and details commoners and their attempt to recreate foods from royalty. Slices of meat were covered in thin films of gold, and the commoners reproduced this by deep frying veal until golden brown. Grammelschmalz, the most interesting food pork fat spread! (I didn’t take a picture since my face was in the food before I even recalled taking out my phone…). We had the most spectacular foods at a local winery/restaurant.

## Milan

New underappreciated country alert! Straight from Vienna, I flew into Milan-Bergamo airport, and roughly an hourlong bus ride later, I was in the grand old city of Milano. Historically, Milan has been the world's capital of commerce and fashion. I think someone mentioned that between Milan and Florence, Leonardo da Vinci’s allegiance can be traced to either of the two cities, sparking a casual rivalry.

My first stop was to visit the old stomping grounds of my friend and professor, Chiara. Situated in the basement, arrays of laser labs were buzzing away with students constructing the next ultrafast laser setups. Here, I had to work on some of the work I neglected back in Belgium, like studying for Dutch and my seminar I have to give later this month. Before you knew it, it was lunchtime, and the weather could not have been more impeccable. Something to note is that in Italy, everything shuts down from 12 to 2pm for lunch. Students here were seen enjoying the early Spring sun, soaking on all the sunlight they could get. The weather was truly bliss during my visit and may it continue for the rest of the year!

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_terrace.jpeg">

The Duomo and just Milan Central area were my stops the next day. The iconic square and the adjacent shopping area were just so grand I don’t even remember any square that could compare. Today was a strolling day and I just did my usual walking tour of the city, which I strongly believe is the best way to explore any city!

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_duomo.jpeg">

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_shopping.jpeg">

The Villa Necchi was a historic mansion built by renowned architect Piero Portaluppi in the 1930s(?). Houses of aristocratic Milanese citizens were known for their timeless designs (the house felt like it was designed yesterday!) and not-too-over-the-top embellishings. The house's elements blended nicely with the adjacent lawn and greenery. Also, the fascinating part about the house was that it also served as a collection of artworks. Among the many pieces of collections, the house had a few Picasso originals hanging in the Prince’s bedroom. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_villa.jpeg">

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_villa1.jpeg">

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_villa2.jpeg">

Ok, I know this is probably a cliché opinion but the food in Italy was just simply the highlight of my visit. Here, I had almost everything Milanese-style! and gorged myself on all the pastries and pizzas widely available in the city. 

Milanese Pizza – One particular thing I enjoyed the most, cold or hot, was the Milanese Pizza. Chiara mentioned that Milanese pizza is typically thicker, with a focaccia-like crust and light toppings. What made it truly Milanese was frying the pizza in the pizza pan with olive oil and water that created a crispy-like texture on the crust. Amazingly tasty. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_pizza.jpeg">

Milanese Schnitzel – I know I had schnitzel in Austria but I had to try the Milanese schnitzel to compare the two! Viennese schnitzel was thin, coated with lemon juice, and deep-fried lightly. For the lack of a better comparison, the veal was fried like a thin chicken katsu! A Milanese schnitzel was a thicker veal cut, breaded with thicker breading, and served with cream/butter. There was more bite to the Milanese schnitzel, and part of me thinks the cut of the veal is the highlight, unlike the crust in a Viennese dish. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_milanschnitzel.jpeg">

Also, what paired nicely with the schnitzel was an appetizer of cod paste and a 2019 wine (I was informed that 2019 was a very good year for grape production). I must admit that I do not know how to appreciate wine, but in this meal, I made my best effort to appreciate the subtle cherry-like tones in my wine!!!

Chinatown – Something I learned while visiting Chinatown was that Milan has a vibrant Asian neighborhood. Shocking. Even more impressive was that the local Milanese would snack on things I had never seen other Westerners snack on, like pickled tofu, fried skewers, and Chinese wraps. It was a long time since I had a bao and zongzi as good as this; I could almost cry, truly. 

<img src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/5/5_zongzi.jpeg">

Pasteria – Manitozzo, Panino, Pannetone, and gelato were the other favorite items I ate during my trip. There are no other words I can use to describe, so here are some pictures.
