Hello team, it’s been a year since I’ve posted but here are some major updates:


## 1. My venture capital part-time stint, a rollercoaster that is always going up

I had the immense privilege to work at a boutique venture capital firm this past year. While it didn’t necessarily convert to a full-time offer, I got an insider’s look into how early-stage venture capital works. Many folks I’ve worked with have cautioned against full-time venture as a career, since the field is overdue for a major shakeup and venture capital funds are a saturated space. Some takeaways from my time here were that: 

1. VC isn’t what everyone makes it out to be, and right now, in the so-called age of AI, much remains to be seen about whether VC funds are jumping on a hype train or doing their due diligence on the company fundamentals themselves. My firm didn’t quite go this route, but all the startup pitches I’ve watched and heard were throwing all the business-savvy terms you could hear. Very routinely, words like ‘SaaS’, ’TAM’, and ‘ARR’ were thrown around without real indications of what they actually represent. 

2. I think the consensus that there are more VC funds than good companies is a very real problem. While my firm was mostly a generalist firm, there were many specialist funds that focused primarily on the AI startup boom and the promised returns. I think I am very much in line with the consensus that, right now, the level of VC funding doesn’t necessarily align well with the number of startups spun out of university labs (at least in deep tech). Also, based on what I gathered, 2020 was a great year for many VC funds, as institutional partners had excess capital to invest. But since then, I have heard murmurs that the biotech area has largely been frozen and difficult to access. If broad sectors are having a hard time being ‘liquid’ (when a startup gets acquired, having biotech companies doing well without having to do their Series E raise, and making transformational changes to human health), then the VC engine eventually comes to a stop. I am not sure what it will take to get this moving again, but so far, other areas of deep tech are still chugging along. 

3. That said, the most important realization from this was that maybe the founder’s life does not seem too bad. Many deep tech founders spin out from their PhD or post-doc work, and strategies I've learned from watching this unfold have definitely made me realize this is truly an experience everyone should have. There is enough funding out there to start a company that, if based on a real beneficial tech, one would not have to invest their own money. The firm I worked for made me realize that, in deep tech at least, the sky’s the limit in starting a company, and I want to do exactly that :)

4. Analyzed many different companies and have a good sense of some of the exciting sectors being developed right now. If I were to put a finger on a sector to keep an eye on, it would probably be space tech. Space is about to get a whole lot busier real soon and real fast. The looming SpaceX IPO this year is indicative of what’s to come and of the broad interest in having a reliable space launch program. 

5. Finally, this isn’t a goodbye, but more of a see you later for VC. I still believe that lots of good work needs to be done in bringing advanced, deep tech towards commercialization, but right now, I believe my best work can still be done in the lab, and hopefully soon, in the commercialization space. 


## 2. Putting on my project manager hat at work

A new project started for work and I am quite thrilled since I get to work on my favorite material and complement it with a field I am still learning more about. So far, it has been a lot of troubleshooting with my Erasmus student intern, but hopefully things will pick up and I will be able to do meaningful chemistry and deposit amplifier thin films. I (would think) that I might be the only one suited to do this since this combines aspects of spectroscopy, materials development, chemistry, cleanroom fabrication, and device characterization. It is kind of the most interdisciplinary project I’ve been tasked with so far, but the project is also giving me somewhat full autonomy on how I spend the project budget, lead different studies, and conduct experiments. Stay tuned for more of this rollercoaster.

## 4. Jazz from the bottom up

Re-immersing myself in music theory before jazz piano. Reading chords is easy, but knowing how to maneuver and jazz up my playing is literally the hardest. So far this month, the music theory theme has been secondary dominants. Secondary dominants are crucial for maneuvering and setting the key in a particular passage. It is used routinely in both classical music and jazz! Jazz pianists use it often to emphasize a particular key or just to throw it in there, just to throw it in there. My favorite resource so far is listening and trying to understand Open Studio Jazz shorts on YouTube.
