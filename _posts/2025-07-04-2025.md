Whew, it’s been a hot second since I last wrote a blog post. I guess one can comment that Spring has been wonderful so far, hence my absence online. In Belgium, rainstorms and overcast skies are typical for the spring season. But this year, which I presume is due to climate change, the weather has been fantastically amazing. Almost every week of spring had 1-2 days of sun. 

Lastly, while I am technically near the end of my BAEF tenure (thank you, BAEF and Optica), activities are still ongoing, I plan to continue this blog, but perhaps in more limited quantities. Since this season was quite busy, I’ll just highlight my five biggest highlights so far:

## 1. Spring Flowers at Ghent and Hallebos

As I’ve mentioned, Spring was absolutely a delight to witness in Belgium. One of my friends, Flor, lives near the Hallebos, which is home to the forest that has rolling meadows of Bluebells. Apparently, this part of Belgium is one of the few locations that host a forest that is protected by the federal government. 

Ghent was also delightful because the Bijlokehof was brimming with cherry-pink petals. The cherry blossom trees that lined the street adjacent to the school were truly an experience, especially on a nice warm day.

## 2. Easter weekend w/ Kris

A few of my 808’ers from Hawaii made the daunting trip from Hawaii to Belgium this year. To welcome them to the land of beers, chocolate, and fries, I HAD to host an Easter brunch at my place. The entire morning felt like a clash of worlds since friends from high school and friends in Belgium met together for brunch. The Belgian gang got a taste of spam and lilikoi pancakes, whereas my Hawaii friends got the baked fresh-croissant-from-Jumbo experience. 

The adventure didn’t stop there because we visited Antwerp and Brugge along the way and took some very, very cute pics.

## 3. China

My roommate, Ward, wanted to join my trip to China since he hadn’t seen China before. From the first day to the last, the entire trip has been an enjoyable experience, which is counter to what I’ve encountered last time I traveled to the far east. Thankfully, the flights that got us there, Hainan Airlines, had a direct flight from Brussels to Shenzhen, and our in-air ordeal was over in less than 12 hours!

Upon landing in Shenzhen, we quickly explored the city and headed straight to my village in Zhongshan. There we spent a few days visiting our ancestral family home, shopping around Shiqi, and exploring neighboring cities. By definition, we visited 3 countries in the Chinese Pearl River Delta region (Macau, China, and Hong Kong), and the trip of 13 days definitely felt like a whirlwind.

I was glad to be able to meet up with family, most importantly, hang out with Grandma, and also be able to show my roommate around China. This trip was probably as much an eye-opening experience as it was for my roommate, since I left for Belgium with a renewed appreciation for the culture and people of China.

## 4. Starting a new side gig

A part-time hobby I recently got into was starting up work at an early-stage venture capital firm. I’ve always done a bit of tech transfer in my PhD studies, but working at a relatively fast-paced venture capital firm was different. I wasn’t just analyzing a piece of tech, I was making sure this tech can hold the promises it’s made against a changing landscape of funding, marketing incentives, and public perception. Things were much more dynamic, and thus, less certain. It’s been an interesting journey so far, and I look forward to learning more in this field since, apparently, to get into VC, you must have interned in VC first.

## 5. Funding status

Lastly, the proposal I have been writing all year long recently came through–I will be staying on as an FWO junior postdoctoral fellow for the next 3 years in Belgium. I am incredibly thankful for this opportunity to work in Belgium, and in the groups of Alberto Curto and Kasper van Gasse, for the next 3 years. It is a terrible time to be a scientist considering the science enterprise in the US, but I am incredibly thankful to have the opportunity to work for Belgium during a time of uncertainty.
